Create an original, fair-play classic detective mystery with a brilliant investigator, a trusted companion, a memorable client, and a closed circle of plausible suspects. Evoke the precision, atmosphere, and deductive pleasure of nineteenth-century consulting-detective fiction without copying any existing title, character, quotation, or plot.

Build a substantial medium-length case with 10–12 meaningful story transitions. Establish the apparent impossibility early, plant every decisive clue before the resolution, and give each suspect a credible motive, opportunity, secret, and distinguishable voice. Include at least two legitimate red herrings whose explanations remain relevant to the human drama. The player must gather evidence, test alibis, notice contradictions, and make deductions; progress must not depend on guessing an unmentioned fact.

The solution must be rational and retrospectively inevitable. Supernatural claims may appear only as misdirection. Make premature or weakly supported accusations consequential, while preserving at least one recoverable route to a complete solution. End with a clear reconstruction explaining the method, motive, timeline, and meaning of the principal clues. Keep danger tense but non-graphic, avoid sexual content, and use only the supplied bounded mechanics vocabulary.
