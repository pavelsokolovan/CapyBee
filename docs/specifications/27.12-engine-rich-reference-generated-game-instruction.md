# Spec 27.12 — Rich Reference Full Generated Game

**Type:** Content and end-to-end implementation specification  
**Parent:** Spec 27.11 — Generated Content Quality and Semantic Consistency  
**Depends on:** Specs 25, 26.1, and 27.1–27.11  
**Baseline:** repository commit `b00c545`, plus the approved designs in Specs 27.4–27.11. The current minimal full-game fixture, pack/rules/scene/place formats, generation catalog and templates, setup and quality resources, fallbacks, CLI/session flows, deterministic provider tests, and result contracts were inspected while writing this specification. The build was not run. Existing uncommitted work was not modified.  
**Status:** Ready for implementation after Spec 27.11  
**Primary outcome:** The repository contains one production-like reference adventure that a player can launch, personalize safely, play through all five full-game phases, inspect through the normal CLI, quit and resume, experience meaningful trusted consequences, and complete with success, partial-success, or failure. Valid provider output, repaired output, and all-authored fallback modes all finish the same trusted game contract.

---

## 1. Purpose

Specs 27.4–27.11 define the machinery needed for a full generated game. The minimal full-game fixture proves individual contracts, but it is intentionally too small and generic to prove that the system feels like a real game.

The reference game must answer the practical question:

> Can a player start CapyBee from the CLI, create a personalized generated adventure, make understandable choices for 15–25 minutes, see consequences, stop and resume, and reach a coherent ending even when generation fails?

Spec 27.12 answers that question with one reviewed adventure, complete authored fallbacks, deterministic provider fixtures, full route scripts, quality corpora, recovery checkpoints, and playtesting gates.

This game is both:

- a playable reference for humans; and
- an end-to-end acceptance asset for the engine.

It is not a replacement for the small fixtures. Unit and contract tests must continue using focused packs where possible.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### 3.1 Goals

1. Add one standalone production-like full generated game pack.
2. Provide 12 authored story slots across the required five phases.
3. Guarantee exactly eight accepted story transitions on every complete route.
4. Provide two investigation openings that converge without erasing their consequences.
5. Include at least four generated cast members and three player-facing objectives.
6. Include three linked challenges with several meaningful trusted effects.
7. Reach distinct success, partial-success, and failure result classes.
8. Keep every route calm, non-punitive, and appropriate for the declared audience.
9. Exercise THEME, SCENARIO, CAST, QUEST, and lazy SCENE generation.
10. Provide complete authored fallback content for every stage and scene.
11. Provide safe setup presets and optional transient personalization.
12. Exercise story plan, continuity facts, dialogue names, objectives, quality repair, session persistence, CLI utility commands, and endings.
13. Supply deterministic scripts and provider responses for all important routes.
14. Define human playtesting criteria for clarity, pacing, agency, and emotional safety.

### 3.2 Non-goals

- Shipping many games or a general pack marketplace.
- Replacing the existing minimal fixtures.
- Supporting multiple locales before the engine has an explicit per-locale fallback-resource contract.
- Adding combat, death, injury, fear-based failure, dice, inventory UI, rewards, accounts, or real-world tasks.
- Letting setup answers alter routes, difficulty, effects, objectives, or endings.
- Making provider availability necessary for completion.
- Creating photorealistic assets, audio, or a graphical client.
- Using live provider output in standard tests.
- Measuring commercial engagement or collecting player telemetry.
- Solving production-provider certification and release operations; Spec 27.13 owns final certification.

## 6. Narrative foundation

### 6.1 Premise

Willow Marsh is preparing for its annual evening lantern festival. The old beacon lens is missing, the wind gate is out of alignment, and water has covered part of the return path. The player joins a small group of neighbors to investigate, repair what can be repaired, and decide whether the festival should shine tonight or wait for safer conditions.

The story is about:

- careful observation;
- asking for and sharing help;
- balancing speed with care;
- accepting that postponing can be a responsible outcome;
- giving credit to a group rather than a single hero.

### 6.2 Immutable story truths

These truths are authored and supplied to quality/ending context:

1. No person or animal is in immediate danger.
2. The festival can be postponed safely.
3. The missing lens is found at the old boathouse.
4. The beacon may be stable, temporary, or left unlit based on trusted choices.
5. Neighbors may cross together or wait safely on the village side.
6. Success requires a stable beacon and a coordinated return.
7. Partial success means useful progress with a smaller or delayed lighting.
8. Failure means a calm postponement with knowledge preserved for tomorrow.
9. No outcome blames, shames, frightens, punishes, or abandons the player.

Generated content may decorate these truths but cannot reverse them.

### 6.3 Tone

Allowed tone tags:

```text
warm
curious
adventurous
reflective
hopeful
```

Every scene includes `warm` or `hopeful`. Tension comes from weather, time, and coordination—not danger, villains, violence, or humiliation.

### 6.4 Story promises

The fallback story plan and all valid provider story plans cover:

1. discover why the beacon cannot be lit;
2. meet neighbors with different useful skills;
3. preserve a detail from the chosen investigation route;
4. repair or safely pause the beacon plan;
5. bring the group to a truthful, supportive ending.

## 7. Cast contract

| Slot | Fallback name | Stable role | Presentation requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| `player-guide` | Rowan | Curious newcomer who notices patterns and asks questions. | Never assigns a real name, age, school, address, or biography to the player. |
| `marsh-keeper` | Mira | Patient keeper of the wetland paths and festival records. | Calm, listens first, never commands or withholds safety information. |
| `young-inventor` | Oren | Practical lantern maker who enjoys testing careful solutions. | Competent but not infallible; shares credit. |
| `heron-guide` | Pip | Observant talking heron who notices wind, water, and reeds. | Light humor, never a pet owned by the player, never mocks mistakes. |

Generated CAST output may change names/descriptions within quality and slot rules, except tests use deterministic names. Every later dialogue line references an allowed slot and resolves to the accepted display name.

Cast quality truths include:

- keeper knows paths/records, not repairs;
- inventor knows mechanisms, not secret outcomes;
- heron observes nature, not human private data;
- player-guide is collaborative, not “chosen” or uniquely powerful.

## 8. Setup and personalization

### 8.1 Questions

| Input | Kind | Required | Values/limit | Routed stages |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| `story-tone` | ENUM | No | `cozy`, `adventurous` | THEME, SCENARIO |
| `interests` | ENUM_SET | No | `animals`, `puzzles`, `making-things`, `nature`; max 2 | THEME, SCENARIO |
| `companion-focus` | ENUM | No | `keeper`, `inventor`, `heron`, `balanced` | SCENARIO |
| `story-wish` | FREE_TEXT | No | max 240 code points | THEME, SCENARIO |

Defaults:

```text
story-tone = cozy
interests = []
companion-focus = balanced
story-wish = omitted
```

### 8.2 Mechanical neutrality

For every valid setup combination:

- scene/choice IDs are identical;
- route graph and story length are identical;
- conditions/effects/objectives/outcomes/result facts are identical;
- call limits and safety rules are identical;
- only theme, plan emphasis, narration, dialogue, and labels may differ;
- `companion-focus` changes narrative attention, not which actor is present in a slot.

Add a parameterized test hashing trusted pack/runtime mechanics across all preset combinations.

### 8.3 Free-text cases

Tests cover:

- safe interest accepted and visibly reflected only in THEME/SCENARIO-derived prose;
- PII scrub/redaction;
- blocked unsafe text;
- cancellation before provider call;
- no-provider mode skips ineffective free text;
- free text absent from scripts, snapshot, session store, logs, quality reports, repair, continuity, and endings;
- resume never asks for it again.

## 10. Trusted mechanics

### 10.1 Counters

| ID | Range | Initial | Display | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `teamwork` | 0–5 | 0 | Yes | How often the group coordinated openly. |
| `care` | 0–6 | 0 | Yes | How often the player chose patient inspection/repair. |

Each option changes a counter by at most 1. No route can exceed bounds.

### 10.2 Flags

```text
took-reed-route
took-orchard-route
shared-discovery
stable-beacon
temporary-beacon
everyone-crossed
festival-ready
```

Route flags are mutually exclusive. `stable-beacon` and `temporary-beacon` are mutually exclusive. Pack validation/tests prove these invariants.

### 10.3 Sets

```text
clues = [reed-song, orchard-mark, lens-note, wind-pattern]
```

- exactly one of `reed-song`/`orchard-mark` is collected;
- `lens-note` is collected at the boathouse;
- `wind-pattern` is collected at the bell garden;
- clues guide presentation/result facts only; they do not unlock hidden provider-created routes.

### 10.4 Objectives

| ID | Visible purpose | Completion |
|---|---|---|
| `find-lens` | Find the missing beacon lens. | Visit `old-boathouse`. |
| `prepare-beacon` | Prepare the beacon safely. | `stable-beacon` or `temporary-beacon`. |
| `bring-neighbors-home` | Help the group return together. | `everyone-crossed = true`. |

At finalization, unresolved objectives receive the trusted terminal state/note required by Spec 27.7. Generated quest prose cannot alter completion.

### 10.5 Outcomes

| Outcome ID | Result class | Trusted meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `radiant-festival` | SUCCESS | Stable beacon, neighbors together, festival begins. |
| `gentle-lighting` | PARTIAL_SUCCESS | Useful progress and a small/safe lighting, with work remaining. |
| `festival-postponed` | FAILURE | Festival is responsibly postponed; clues and repair plan are preserved. |

`FAILURE` is a valid calm game ending and process exit 0.

### 10.6 Result facts

At least these authored facts are available conditionally:

```text
followed-reed-song
protected-orchard
shared-the-lens-plan
decoded-wind-pattern
made-stable-repair
used-temporary-repair
crossed-together
kept-everyone-safe
festival-shone
plan-saved-for-tomorrow
```

Only true conditions select facts. Generated ending prose receives the selected trusted facts and cannot add structured facts.

### 11.4 Resolution

At `beacon-platform`:

| Choice ID | Requirement | Outcome/effects |
|---|---|---|
| `light-together` | stable beacon, everyone crossed, teamwork ≥3 | Set festival ready; end `radiant-festival`. |
| `hold-gentle-lighting` | stable or temporary beacon | End `gentle-lighting`. |
| `postpone-with-plan` | Always enabled | End `festival-postponed`. |

When `light-together` is unavailable, show an authored localized hint based only on trusted conditions. The generated label may not promise success for a disabled option.

### 11.5 Meaningful consequence proof

Tests prove:

- at least one route can reach each outcome;
- not every setup/route automatically reaches success;
- route/clue choices change visible result facts and later continuity;
- careful versus collaborative choices affect status and ending availability;
- a later choice cannot erase the route fact;
- outcome is determined only by authored requirements/effects;
- generated/fallback labels do not change availability or result.

New full-game artifacts use Spec 27.11 schema v3 and quality stamps. The pack itself contains no fixed artifact IDs or engine actor IDs.

## 14. Fallback content requirements

### 14.1 General

Every fallback is polished player-facing content, not placeholder test prose. Each must:

- pass exact structural/reference validation;
- pass Spec 27.11 mandatory and profile quality rules;
- be true for its authored mechanics/context;
- stay within field limits;
- use cast slots and choice IDs exactly;
- provide 1–4 useful continuity facts for non-ending scenes;
- avoid revealing unchosen routes or future outcomes;
- remain coherent when all prior stages also used fallback;
- remain coherent when prior stages were generated/repaired.

### 14.2 Required fallback narrative beats

| Scene | Required fallback beat |
|---|---|
| festival-square | Festival preparations pause; beacon lens is missing; two safe leads appear. |
| reed-bank | Pip connects reed rhythm/water marks to the boathouse route. |
| orchard-path | Wheel marks/seedling care point toward the boathouse. |
| old-boathouse | Group finds the lens and a maintenance note. |
| bell-garden | Bells reveal the wind pattern needed for the gate. |
| wind-gate | Group aligns or measures the gate safely. |
| lantern-workshop | Player chooses stable repair or safe temporary patch. |
| marsh-crossing | Player chooses group crossing or careful path marking while others wait. |
| beacon-platform | Current trusted preparation is summarized without declaring outcome; three authored choices remain truthful. |
| ending-radiant | Stable beacon and group success; shared credit; all required objective states truthful. |
| ending-gentle | Smaller/delayed lighting and remaining work stated honestly; progress valued. |
| ending-postponed | Calm postponement; safety and tomorrow’s plan emphasized; no blame. |

### 14.3 Initial fallbacks

- THEME establishes warm cooperative mystery and allowed tones.
- SCENARIO contains all five phases, all story promises, exact slot/objective/role/tone coverage.
- CAST provides the four fallback members in Section 7.
- QUEST gives clear age-appropriate titles/descriptions for all three trusted objectives.

### 14.4 Ending truth matrix

The pack-quality test evaluates ending fallback objective notes against every reachable terminal state for its ending. If one ending slot can receive materially different objective states, either:

- all notes are explicitly state-neutral and valid for each; or
- split the slot/resource.

For this reference pack, each ending route has a deterministic expected state matrix, and distinct ending resources are required.

## 15. Quality profile and corpus

### 15.1 Profile

Create `willow-marsh-family-en-v1` with:

- `CHILD` audience rules for ages 8–12;
- all five tone profiles;
- truth phrases for three outcomes, three objectives and their reachable terminal states, immutable story truths, stable/temporary/unlit beacon, and group crossing/waiting;
- completion claims;
- control/meta/private-data phrase sets;
- non-shaming/non-pressuring ending phrases;
- generic-title and unresolved-placeholder sets;
- readability and repetition thresholds inside Spec 27.11 bounds.

Profile phrases are reviewed as pack content and tested for boundary/negation cases.

### 15.2 Pack-specific corpus

Add synthetic provider payloads for:

- valid output for every stage and slot;
- advisory-only cozy/adventurous variants;
- repeated narration at bell garden;
- ambiguous workshop choices;
- wrong actor slot dialogue;
- continuity claim that says the lens was not found;
- early completion at wind gate;
- partial ending claiming total success;
- postponed ending blaming the player;
- result fact/objective contradiction;
- repair outputs that fix each blocking case;
- second invalid output that forces fallback.

No corpus case contains personal information or live-provider text.

### 15.3 Quality acceptance

The reference pack fails CI if:

- any authored fallback gains a blocking/advisory finding not explicitly allow-listed and reviewed;
- an expected blocking provider case is accepted;
- a clean provider case falls back;
- threshold changes alter corpus decisions without evaluator/profile version review;
- quality diagnostics contain prose.

The target is zero advisory findings in authored fallbacks.

### 16.3 Provider assertions

## 20. Human playtesting

### 20.1 Participants and sessions

Before calling the reference game complete:

- at least 8 internal/adult proxy sessions focused on usability;
- when approved by the project’s research/safety process, at least 5 supervised target-audience sessions or qualified educator/parent reviews;
- no collection of unnecessary personal data;
- no live-provider test with children until provider/privacy review is complete;
- fallback or reviewed deterministic content is sufficient for early sessions.

This specification does not itself authorize child research or data collection.

### 20.2 Required observations

Measure without hidden telemetry:

- Can the player explain the goal after opening?
- Can they distinguish the two choices at each scene?
- Do they understand status/objectives/map/help?
- Do route clues reappear clearly later?
- Does the middle feel progressive rather than repetitive?
- Are consequences understandable without exposing rules?
- Do disabled ending choices have helpful hints?
- Does partial success feel meaningful?
- Does failure/postponement feel safe and non-punitive?
- Can the player quit and resume without help?
- Is the final result consistent with remembered choices?

### 20.3 Acceptance targets

- 90% of internal sessions identify the main goal without developer explanation.
- 90% distinguish all displayed choice pairs.
- 80% can use `/help` and `/objectives` after one prompt.
- 80% can explain at least one earlier choice reflected in the ending.
- No reviewed ending is perceived as blaming, frightening, or contradictory.
- Median fallback route time is 15–25 minutes at intended reading pace.
- No route exceeds 30 minutes in the fallback script/read-aloud estimate.
- Every reported contradiction, blocked progression, misleading choice, privacy issue, or unsafe tone is a release blocker until triaged and covered by a regression test.

### 20.4 Feedback artifact

Store only content-free review records in repository documentation:

- session/review ID;
- pack version;
- route/outcome;
- deterministic/provider/fallback mode;
- issue codes and severity;
- resolution and regression-test link.

Do not commit names, contact details, raw child statements, setup text, or provider conversations.
